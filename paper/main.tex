\begin{abstract}
We study short solutions of the \Sq{N} Rubik's Square and the \Cu{N} Rubik's Cube for every size we can run, on one open
engine, with nine solvers: our reproductions of IDA* with pattern databases, reduction with Kociemba's two-phase algorithm,
Demaine et al.'s parallel cluster algorithm, the CayleyPy per-size network and, from its write-up, the KMCoders pipeline that
produced the strongest public large-cube solutions in Kaggle's Santa 2023 competition; and four new methods. \FiveC schedules
Demaine's bulk commutators by net gain: a bulk move is applied to a whole block of layers when the pieces it fixes per move beat
the best single-cluster fix, even if some cells of the block do not need it. On random cubes it is the shortest of our methods
from \Cu{5} to \Cu{20} (\CuTwentyHTMFiveC{} moves on the \Cu{20}, \FiveCGainTwenty\% fewer than Demaine's algorithm), with a lead
that grows with $N$ and is significant from $N=5$ on 20 states per size. \Mseven is a symmetry portfolio: the 48 rotations and
reflections of the cube turn one scramble into 48 equivalent inputs to KMCoders' deterministic solver, and every answer maps back
with the same length. On the Santa 2023 \Cu{4} puzzles it averages \MsevenBestFour{} quarter turns against \MsevenPubFour{} for
KMCoders' published best-of-many solutions and \MsevenOneFour{} for one run of their program, and with an optimal central
3$\times$3$\times$3 it is shorter than the published solution on all \MsevenNFive{} \Cu{5} puzzles we ran (\MsevenBestFive{} against
\MsevenPubFive{} on average). Two learned methods decompose the puzzle into cubie
clusters shared by all sizes: a staged beam search that solved every random state up to $N=20$, and a cluster-token transformer
whose distance estimates carry over to unseen sizes. Finally, a counting lower bound, calibrated on Squares whose exact God's
numbers we compute (\GodSquareThree, \GodSquareFour, \GodSquareFive{} for $N=3,4,5$), shows how far everything is from optimal: the
published Santa lengths are \PubOverBoundFour{}$\times$ the bound at $N=4$ and \PubOverBoundThirtyThree{}$\times$ at $N=33$, a gap
that grows like $\log N$. We release the engine, the benchmarks, every solver and every result file.
\end{abstract}
