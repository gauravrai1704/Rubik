\begin{abstract}
We study short solutions of the \Sq{N} Rubik's Square and the \Cu{N} Rubik's Cube on one open engine, with eight solvers: our
reproductions of IDA* with pattern databases, reduction with Kociemba's two-phase algorithm, Demaine et al.'s parallel cluster
algorithm, the CayleyPy per-size network and, from its write-up, the KMCoders pipeline that produced the strongest public large-cube
solutions in Kaggle's Santa 2023 competition; and three new methods. Our main method, \Mseven, is a symmetry portfolio: the
rotations and reflections of the puzzle turn one scramble into many equivalent inputs to a deterministic solver, and every answer
maps back to a solution of the original state with the same length. Around KMCoders' own solver, with an optimal central
3$\times$3$\times$3 stage, it is the best method at \MsevenRedCube{} of the \SizesCube{} Santa 2023 cube sizes, shorter in total than
KMCoders' published best-of-many solutions on the puzzles it ran (\MsevenAllTotal{} against \MsevenAllPubTotal{} quarter turns); around
our strongest Square solvers it is the best method at \MsevenRedSquare{} of the \SizesSquare{} Square sizes. \FiveC schedules Demaine's
bulk commutators by net gain: a bulk move is applied to a whole block of layers when the pieces it fixes per move beat the best
single-cluster fix. It is the strongest single solver on large Squares and, on random cubes, \FiveCGainTwenty\% shorter than Demaine's
algorithm at \Cu{20}, with a lead that grows with $N$. \FiveA, a cluster-token transformer trained on small Squares only, solves Squares of
sizes it never saw. Finally, a counting lower bound, calibrated on Squares whose exact God's numbers we compute (\GodSquareThree,
\GodSquareFour, \GodSquareFive{} for $N=3,4,5$), shows how far everything is from optimal: the published Santa lengths are
\PubOverBoundFour{}$\times$ the bound at $N=4$ and \PubOverBoundThirtyThree{}$\times$ at $N=33$, a gap that grows like $\log N$. We release
the engine, the benchmarks, every solver and every result file.
\end{abstract}
