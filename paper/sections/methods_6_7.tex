\subsection{Method 6: the KMCoders pipeline from its write-up (our reproduction)}
\label{sec:m6}

\emph{Origin.} The cube part of KMCoders' Santa 2023 solution~\cite{kmcoders2024santa}, implemented from their written
description only, before we looked at their code. It targets the exact Santa goal and the Santa (QTM) metric.

\emph{The insertion identity.} All later stages rest on one observation of KMCoders. For a move sequence
$a_0\dots a_{T-1}$ let $\mathrm{suf}_t$ be the permutation of the suffix $a_t\dots a_{T-1}$ and $\mathrm{inv}_t$ its
inverse. Inserting a sequence $B$ before move $t$ turns the final state $F$ into $F[C]$ with
\begin{equation}
C=\mathrm{inv}_t\circ B\circ\mathrm{suf}_t ,
\label{eq:insert}
\end{equation}
so a sequence that acts on a few stickers at time $t$ acts on the stickers $\mathrm{inv}_t[\mathrm{support}(B)]$ of the
final state. With all suffix permutations stored, scoring a candidate at every time is a gather over $\mathrm{inv}$, with no
replay; inserting where the neighbouring moves cancel the candidate's first or last move makes it shorter.

\emph{Steps.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Special parts.} Middle-slice turns bring odd cubes' fixed centers home (the Santa goal is exact). For even $N$
  the corners form a \Cu{2}, solved optimally in QTM with \Mone's corner pattern database (this matches KMCoders' \Cu{2}
  lengths exactly); for odd $N$ the corners, middle edges and fixed centers form a \Cu{3}, solved by Kociemba's two-phase
  algorithm (KMCoders used an optimal solver).
\item \emph{Parity.} Every wing cluster with an odd permutation gets one inner-slice quarter turn.
\item \emph{Annealing.} Simulated annealing over the move sequence with objective
  $\text{length (QTM)}+\alpha\cdot\#\text{wrong stickers}$ ($\alpha=2.5$, $6000N^2$ iterations, temperature $2\to0.05$).
  Only inner, non-middle slice quarter turns may be edited, so special parts stay solved; neighbours insert two moves of
  one parity class at two times, remove two such moves, move one move to another time, change one move within its class,
  or swap two adjacent moves. Each keeps every cluster's permutation even and is scored exactly in $O(6N^2)$ with
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:insert}.
\item \emph{Commutators.} Greedy insertion of 4-move commutators $[x,y]$ of two slices (inner--inner, inner--face and
  face--inner), each a pair of 3-cycles: at each step the (commutator, time) pair with the best pieces fixed per move, net of
  cancellations, over 256 sampled times.
\item \emph{3-cycles.} Every cluster is finished with pure 3-cycles from a library that is QTM-optimal per 3-cycle (built
  by breadth-first search over conjugating setups), each inserted at the time where it is cheapest.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Msix: KMCoders pipeline (our reproduction from the write-up)}
\label{alg:m6}
\small
\KwIn{state $s$ (exact goal $s^\ast$)}
\KwOut{move sequence $a$ (quarter turns)}
$a\leftarrow$ orientation moves $\Vert$ optimal special-part solution $\Vert$ wing parity fixes\;
$a\leftarrow$ \textsc{Anneal}$(a)$ over parity-preserving inner-slice edits, objective $|a|+\alpha\cdot\mathrm{wrong}(s\cdot a)$\;
\While{many stickers wrong}{
  $(B,t)\leftarrow\arg\max$ gain$(B,t)/\mathrm{cost}(B,t)$ over 4-move commutators $B$, sampled times $t$ \tcp*{Eq.~\eqref{eq:insert}}
  insert $B$ before move $t$; update $\mathrm{suf},\mathrm{inv}$\;}
\For{each cluster $c$ until solved}{
  pick a 3-cycle $B$ of $c$'s library and the time $t$ where it is cheapest; insert it\;}
\Return $a$\;
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Cost.} Storing $\mathrm{suf}_t$ and $\mathrm{inv}_t$ for all $T$ times takes $O(T\cdot6N^2)$ memory (a few megabytes at
$N=10$); an insertion rebuilds the suffixes before it in $O(t\cdot6N^2)$. One greedy round scores $O(\text{times}\times
\text{candidates})$ gathers. Every 3-cycle fixes $O(1)$ pieces, so solutions have $\Theta(N^2)$ moves.
\emph{What it leaves out.} Comparing with KMCoders' code shows what the write-up does not describe: their stage 3 also applies
bulk commutators $[D_I,R_J]$ of whole slice sets $I$, $J$ chosen by annealing (Demaine-style parallel moves), their final stage
counts the exact minimum number of 3-cycles each cluster still needs and inserts only 3-cycles that lower it, with a beam of
$10^5$ sequences for small $N$, and they solve odd cubes' central \Cu{3} optimally.

\subsection{Method 7: a symmetry portfolio around deterministic solvers (new, main method)}
\label{sec:m7}

\emph{Idea.} A deterministic solver that is far from optimal is not equally far on every input: KMCoders' program, run once
on the same scramble seen from different sides, returns solutions whose lengths differ by \MsevenSpreadMinFour{}--\MsevenSpreadMaxFour{} quarter turns on one \Cu{4} (Figure~\ref{fig:m7copies}).
The 48 symmetries of the cube turn one scramble into 48 equivalent inputs, and every answer maps back to a solution of the
original state of the same length. \Mseven runs the solver on $k$ copies and keeps the shortest. The same holds for the Rubik's
Square, whose 4 rotations and their mirror images give 8 copies; there the inner solvers are our own.

\emph{Symmetric copies.} Let $R$ be a sticker permutation of one of the 24 rotations of the cube, or of one of their 24
mirror images (rotation composed with the reflection $x\mapsto-x$). Let $\sigma$ be the colour relabelling with
$\sigma(s^\ast[R])=s^\ast$ for the solved state $s^\ast$. The copy of a state $s$ is $s'=\sigma(s[R])$.

\textbf{Proposition.} \emph{For every move $q$ there is a move $\phi_R(q)$ of the same cost with
$R\circ q\circ R^{-1}=\phi_R(q)$ (as sticker permutations); for a rotation $\phi_R$ maps a quarter turn of a slice to a quarter
turn of the image slice, for a mirror image to the opposite quarter turn of the mirrored slice. Hence if $q_1\dots q_L$ solves
$s'$ exactly, then $\phi_R(q_1)\dots\phi_R(q_L)$ solves $s$ exactly, with the same QTM, HTM and STM.}

\emph{Proof sketch.} A rotation or reflection maps every slab of the cube onto a slab, so conjugating a slab turn by $R$ gives
a turn of the image slab by the same angle, with the sense reversed for a reflection; the move set is closed under this map and
costs are preserved. Applying the moves to $s'$ is the gather $s'[q_1\circ\dots\circ q_L]$; substituting $s'=\sigma(s[R])$ and
$q_i=R^{-1}\phi_R(q_i)R$ telescopes the inner $R^{-1}R$ pairs, so the result is $\sigma(s[\phi_R(q_1)\dots\phi_R(q_L)][R])$, which
equals $s^\ast$ exactly when $s[\phi_R(q_1)\dots\phi_R(q_L)]=s^\ast$ (because $\sigma(s^\ast[R])=s^\ast$ and $R$ is a bijection).
The harness verifies every mapped-back solution independently. $\square$

\emph{Steps.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Inner solver.} KMCoders' program (B2) with three settings: their beam variant (\texttt{solve\_cube\_beam}), which their
  write-up recommends for small $N$; 1M instead of 20M annealing iterations in their second stage, which takes about three
  quarters of a \Cu{4} run and did not change lengths measurably at 0.5M, 2M or 20M iterations; and, for odd $N$, an optimal
  quarter-turn central \Cu{3} from \texttt{nxopt} (Kociemba's answer only defines the position; \texttt{nxopt} solves it
  optimally), supplied through their cache file. On Santa puzzle 210 this stage drops from 34 to 24 quarter turns.
\item \emph{Copies.} The identity, its mirror image, then the other rotations each followed by its mirror image, up to $k$
  (48 for the cube, 8 for the Square).
\item \emph{Square.} The inner solvers are the strongest we have at each size, and every copy is solved by each of them: \Mfour's
  per-size network ($W{=}4096$) and \FiveC for $N\le8$, \FiveC and \Mthree above (\Mfour has no models beyond $N=8$). The goal is any
  solved orientation, which the Proposition also preserves because the symmetries permute the solved orientations.
\item \emph{Portfolio.} Run the inner solver on each copy (three in parallel), map each answer back, verify it, and keep the
  shortest in QTM. For the \Cu{4} we also take the shortest over an 8-copy portfolio of the default program (greedy, 20M
  iterations).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Mseven: symmetry portfolio}
\label{alg:m7}
\small
\KwIn{state $s$; inner solver $\mathcal{S}$; number of copies $k$}
\KwOut{solution of $s$}
$\mathcal{R}\leftarrow$ (identity, mirror, $R_1$, mirror$(R_1)$, \dots) truncated to $k$\;
best $\leftarrow\bot$\;
\ParallelFor{\upshape each $R\in\mathcal{R}$}{
  $s'\leftarrow\sigma_R(s[R])$\;
  $q\leftarrow\mathcal{S}(s')$ \tcp*{KMCoders' program, beam variant, short stage 2, optimal \Cu{3} stage}
  $\mu\leftarrow\phi_R(q)$\;
  \lIf{$s\cdot\mu=s^\ast$ \textbf{and} $|\mu|_{\mathrm{QTM}}<|\text{best}|_{\mathrm{QTM}}$}{best $\leftarrow\mu$}}
\Return best\;
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Cost.} $k$ times the inner solver's CPU time; memory is the inner solver's (plus \texttt{nxopt}'s 1.42\,GB table, shared).
Measured: \MsevenCpuMinFour{} CPU-minutes per \Cu{4} puzzle for 48 copies and \MsevenCpuMinFive{} per \Cu{5} puzzle for 24
copies, on a shared 4-core machine. Moves: as KMCoders' method, $\Theta(N^2)$ ($\approx4.4N^2$ in the published data); the
portfolio lowers the constant, not the exponent.

\emph{What did not help.} Two attempts to improve KMCoders' finished solutions directly found nothing: simulated annealing
over the free moves of a finished solution (\Msix's annealer with a large $\alpha$) removed no move on two \Cu{4} puzzles, and a
ruin-and-recreate local search~\cite{shaw1998lns} inside their stage 3 (delete the inner-slice moves of a random window in
parity-safe pairs, let their greedy repair it, accept if not longer) found no shorter solution in 21 rounds. Their solutions are
locally tight; diversity of the whole run, not local repair, is what pays.
