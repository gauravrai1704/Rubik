Rubik's Cube · N×N×N
6 faces of N×N stickers. Any one of the 3N slices turns by 90°, 180° or 270°.
9N movesa quarter turn needs 3 more to undosame-axis slices commute

The key tool: the commutator c r c r

flip column c, row r, column c, row r  →  a 3-cycle of the crossing and its two mirror cells; everything else is restored

The order: outside in

Clusters on a 5×5×5

Pure 3-cycle

Moves exactly three pieces of one cluster and nothing else. Built from commutators such as [A, B C B′] plus setup moves: all 4,048 per cluster are generated automatically.

Reduction pipeline

used by Reduction, Demaine and NetGain
1
Orient. For Santa, bring the fixed centers home: the goal is one exact orientation.
2
Wing parity. One inner-slice quarter turn per odd wing cluster: 3-cycles only make even permutations.
3
Centers. Pure 3-cycles until every center cluster is solved.
4
3×3×3 core. Corners, middle edges and fixed centers now act like a 3×3×3: Kociemba's two-phase solves them. Outer turns keep centers on their faces.
5
Wings. Pure 3-cycles; they touch nothing else.
The methods differ in steps 3 and 5: Reduction fixes one cluster at a time, Demaine and NetGain fix many at once with one shared move sequence.

Demaine: every cell must need it

Best unanimous block {1,3}×{0,2,5}: 6 clusters, 18 pieces for 2·2+2·3+4 = 14 moves → 1.3 pieces/move. Rows 4 and 6 are left out because one cell each does not need it; as clusters diverge, such blocks shrink to single cells.

NetGain: apply the block when it pays

Block {1,3,4,6}×{0,2,5}: 10 helped, 1 neutral, 1 slightly hurt → 29 pieces for 2·4+2·3+4 = 18 moves → 1.6 pieces/move, better than the best single fix (λ).
choose X, Y to maximise  Σ(i,j)∈X×Y gain(i,j) − λ·(2|X| + 2|Y| + 4 + 2|setup|)
λ = pieces per move of the best single 3-cycle now. Measured on a random 20×20×20: Demaine's center rounds fix one cluster in 244 of 292; NetGain's are bulk in 229 of 333; 4,925 vs 6,612 moves.
